Penetration-Defense-Checklist

American hospitals, water utilities, energy grids, telecoms, and companies of every size are being hit by state-linked hackers out of China, Russia, Iran, and North Korea — right now, not hypothetically. This is a free, source-linked checklist to help defenders close the doors those attacks actually walk through.

MIT Licensed ~58 min read 13 sections · 135+ checklist items

Over the past two years, foreign state-linked hacking groups and the ransomware crews operating with their tolerance have breached U.S. telecoms, hospitals, water utilities, energy infrastructure, and companies large and small — not as isolated incidents, but as a sustained, ongoing campaign. A Chinese state-sponsored group sat inside American telecom networks for months, reading call records and intercepting communications tied to government and political activity. Russian and Iranian-linked actors have gone after water treatment plants, power infrastructure, and hospitals, sometimes for espionage, sometimes just to prove they could. None of these intrusions required something exotic. They started with an exposed industrial controller still running its factory-default password, an edge device that hadn't been patched, a phished credential, or a vendor with weak controls sitting inside a network it shouldn't have had that much access to.

This checklist exists because the people responsible for defending American infrastructure and companies — IT teams, security engineers, small-business owners without a dedicated security staff — deserve a resource that treats this as the serious, ongoing problem it is, without requiring a six-figure consulting budget to act on it. Every item below starts from a real, documented attack pattern and ends at a concrete, checkable control, sourced to CISA, MITRE, NIST, or OWASP rather than a blog post. Work it top to bottom before your organization becomes the next line item in a breach report, or jump straight to the section that matches what you're responsible for hardening today.

Identity & Active Directory Defense

09

Defending U.S. Infrastructure & Companies From Foreign Hacking

State-linked hacking groups out of China, Russia, Iran, and North Korea, along with the ransomware crews operating with their tolerance, have moved from opportunistic crime to sustained campaigns against American water utilities, energy grids, telecoms, hospitals, and companies of every size. None of these intrusions started with something exotic — they started with an exposed PLC, an unpatched edge device, a phished credential, or a trusted vendor with weak controls. Every checkbox below is a documented pattern from CSIS's Significant Cyber Incidents timeline, paraphrased into the specific control that would have closed the door. Work through it as a standing defense posture, not a one-time read — foreign intrusion campaigns are ongoing, and the checklist should be too.

10

Social Engineering & Phishing Defense

The MGM Resorts breach, the Trump campaign hack, and countless ransomware intrusions never touched a technical vulnerability at all — they talked their way past a human. This section hardens the people layer directly.

11

Mobile Device & BYOD Security

Phones and personal laptops routinely hold as much sensitive access as a desktop, with far less oversight. Chinese hackers reportedly reached the personal cellphones of a presidential campaign in 2024 — mobile is not a lesser attack surface.

12

Physical Security & Facility Access

The tracking chip planted in a general's conference badge and the residential webcams hijacked to scout a target before a strike are reminders in Section 9 that physical and digital security are the same discipline. This section covers the physical side directly.

13

Third-Party & Supply Chain Risk Management

The Sisense, Snowflake, XZ Utils, and Polyfill.io incidents referenced earlier all share one root cause: an outside party you trusted became the way in. This section makes vendor risk an ongoing program, not a one-time questionnaire.
